\title{Inpainting over the cracks: challenges of applying pre-merger searches for massive black hole binaries to realistic LISA datasets}

\author{Gareth {Cabourn Davies}}
\email{gareth.cabourn-davies@port.ac.uk}
\affiliation{\portsmouth}
\author{Ian Harry}%
\email{ian.harry@port.ac.uk}
\affiliation{\portsmouth}

\begin{abstract}
    Pre-merger detection of massive black hole binaries by the Large 
    Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) will provide the best chance to
    follow up potential signals, maximising the science of LISA and multi-messenger partners.
    Providing early warning and locations in the weeks to months before merger
    is critical to observe galactic mergers happening in real time. 
    This paper shows results of applying a matched filtering pre-merger
    search to the LISA Data Challenge dataset 2a: Sangria-HM. This matched filtering
    search is based on a zero-latency filter which does not
    require data into the future. We find that pre-merger searches are susceptible
    to significant artefacts which can render the search ineffective for close-in-time
    mergers. Through signal subtraction, we show how we can mitigate these effects.
    We also demonstrate pre-merger searches using inpainting, which can act without data in
    the future, but are also resilient to data artefacts such as gaps or glitches.
\end{abstract}
