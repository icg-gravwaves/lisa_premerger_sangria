\section{The Sangria HM Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

We test our search methods on the LISA Data Challenge dataset~\cite{Baghi:2022ucj, LDC_WEBSITE}.
The LISA Data Challenges are designed to provide comprehensively modelled data as an introduction
to LISA data analysis. The dataset considered here is Challenge 2a, also known as
\textit{Sangria-HM}~\cite{LDC_WEBSITE_SANGRIA_HM}.
This dataset is similar to Challenge 2: Sangria~\cite{LDC_WEBSITE_SANGRIA, le_jeune_2022_7132178};
it contains exactly the same sources but uses a different waveform model, \texttt{PhenomHM}~\cite{Garcia-Quiros:2020qpx}.

The Sangria-HM dataset contains Gaussian instrumental noise and simulations of astrophysical sources; a foreground of
Galactic binaries and \ac{MBHB} systems, both with parameters derived from astrophysical models.
Here we use the training dataset, where we know the source parameters of signals, the model of instrumental noise used,
and the form of LISA's orbit used to generate the data.

In applying our pre-merger search to the Sangria dataset, our first consideration is to check whether
the data has similar enough properties to our previously-assumed noise that our approach is directly transferable.
The main difference between the noise assumptions we made before and the Sangria-HM dataset is the
inclusion of a Galactic binary foreground made up of millions of white dwarf binaries in our galaxy; these are continuous sources, and
occupy the same frequency space as mergers of \acp{MBHB}.
Many of the white dwarf systems are not expected to be resolvable, and so must be considered an extra source of noise for \ac{MBHB}
searches.
In fact we treat all white dwarf signals as noise in this paper, whether they would be resolved or not, and the
search is based on the `day-one' power-spectral density of the data.
The contribution of this `noise' will vary throughout the year, as LISA's most sensitive direction is oriented
towards or away from the galactic centre.
This means that as the white dwarf sources are resolved and can be removed from the data, or when LISA is optimally oriented,
\ac{MBHB} searches will become more sensitive, but effects of that are not considered here.

The white dwarf confusion noise overlaps with \ac{MBHB} mergers in frequency space. But our search is not \textit{for} the mergers,
rather the inspiral stage of the signal, many days before merger, and so we consider whether the frequency overlaps with the
particular times-before-merger for our signals.

Figure~\ref{fig:psds} shows the power-spectral density directly estimated from the data, the modelled power-spectral density with and without Galactic binary foreground noise, and an example
characteristic strain curve for Signal 2 in the Sangria-HM dataset (See Section~\ref{subsec:expected_snr}
for a description).

noise begins to overlap the \ac{MBHB}.


\subsection{Smoothing the power spectral density}
\label{subsec:smoothing}
At $\sim60$\,mHz, we see a dip in the power-spectral density, this is an artifact where gravitational waves at this frequency have a wavelength
of $5\times10^{9}$\,m, which is exactly double the arm length of LISA, and is not physical.
The problem arises here that it is effectively an inverse delta function in the power-spectral density, and delta functions in the
power-spectral density lead to broad-band noise--known as spectral leakage--in the time domain.

To remove this noise, we smooth over it. We identify peaks in the model either side of the dip, and draw a line between them,
so that this is not introducing discontinuities in the spectrum, we use a Hann window to smoothly transition between the power-spectral density
and the straight line. We illustrate this in Figure~\ref{fig:smoothing}.

\subsection{Expected signal-to-noise ratios for signals}
\label{subsec:expected_snr}
For the example characteristic strain in Figure~\ref{fig:psds}, we use the \textit{characteristic
strain}~\cite{Moore:2014lga} for the example of Signal 2.
Characteristic strain has a neat property; when the area between the characteristic strain and the
power-spectral density in the frequency domain is calculated, this is the expected SNR for the signal.

Characteristic strains at the times-before-merger that we consider are shown in
Table~\ref{tab:characteristic_strain_snr}.
We compute this for each signal in the Sangria-HM dataset, with cutoffs set according to the
time--frequency evolution expected for an inspiralling \ac{MBHB} signal.
We see that Signal 1 is unlikely to be seen even 0.5 days pre-merger, and that only the loudest signals -
0, 3, 4, 10, 11 and possibly 12, 13 - would be seen 4 days or more before merger.
