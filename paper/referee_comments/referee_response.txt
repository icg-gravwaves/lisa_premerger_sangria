3. Page 2.
The degree of separation between the frequency content of premerger MBHBs and the other resolvable sources depends on the masses of MBHBs.
It would be useful to illustrate and explain this.
Provided enough separation, we agree that the premerger detection of MBHBs is, in principle, not a global-fit problem, however, we wonder, for this to hold true in practice, whether there are requirements on the technique or not.
For instance, on page 7, the authors match zero padded data with shifting templates, and since there is no lower bound to the advance in time for the merger, the templates would a priori contain the merger (this is the case for ground-based detector researches and the method described in 2504.11322 for LISA); as a result, the normalization involves frequency content of MBHBs that actually overlap with other resolvable sources.
Would this invalidate the "not a global-fit problem" claim for this technique?

> We have added a new appendix to address this point.

We find that ignoring resolvable galactic binary sources has a small (less than 10 percent) effect on recovered SNR for all Sangria sources up to one day before merger, and have shown the effect that the signal mass has on this loss of power.
Therefore the global fit problem would be useful for optimisation in this technique, but would not invalidate the technique.

We have updated the text to replace:
This is important because it indicates that premerger detection of MBHB is not a ``Global Fit'' problem.

with

This is important because it indicates that premerger detection of MBHB can be run immediately at the start of the mission.

And added:

This point is explored further in Appendix A.

This indicated in the manuscript with strikethrough and bold text

6. Table II.
The total mass of the template bank construction is limited to 10^6 to 2x10^7 solar masses.
This is not a wide range for MBHBs detectable by LISA.
Would enlarging the range to the full population of MBHBs detectable by LISA increase the bank size to a computationally unmanageable scale?
Same question to the mass ratio.

> The choices made here were designed to include the cover the parameter ranges used in the Sangria-HM dataset. We agree that the physical range, while not well known, could well include both higher- and lower-mass signals.

Nevertheless the methods here can definitely be scaled to the full range of MBHBs without reaching computationally unmanageable scales. Increasing the total mass will not meaningfully affect bank size, though such sources will merge more quickly and not really be detectables weeks / days out. Lower mass sources will increase the bank size. We leave a discussion of performance in the lower mass space for a separate paper. One would want to not only explore bank size, but also how important prompt detection is and the methods to use for it. If a signal is observable over months, one can happily taper the last hour(s) of data without loss of signal power.

We have added to the Table 2 caption:

The choices made here were designed to include the full set of Sangria-HM dataset signals, which range in total mass from $1.6\times 10^6 M_\odot$ to $9.6\times 10^6 M_\odot$, mass ratio ranges from $1.03$ to $2.75$, and component spins in the z-direction range from $-0.7$ to $0.8$. 

and to the text we have added:
These parameter choices exclude some of the massive black hole binary parameter space, particularly at lower masses.
This could be a very interesting region to explore, but is not explored in the context of proving the technique of this work.

This probably requires clarification and elaboration: after all, the only factor that changes with the inclusion of confusion is higher noise level at high frequencies (with respect to pre-merger MBHBs).
Shouldn't this explain the need for more templates?

> Due to the noise, less power can be recovered at high frequencies, so the fraction of total power that is recovered from the signal at lower frequencies increases. This broadens the "noise moments" (in the sense of traditional template bank papers), or increases the bandwidth of the signal.
We have reworded this text to:

We can see that significantly more templates are required when the confusion noise is included, presumably due to the reduced noise at high frequency resulting in an increased bandwidth of the template, which is the dominant factor in template bank size~\cite{https://inspirehep.net/literature/402088 https://inspirehep.net/literature/714134}.
