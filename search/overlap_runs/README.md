## Overlap runs

Here we calculate the SNR we should get as a function of time.
The issue we see is that the pre-merger search produces a spike at the time of the merger, and another one later.
For example, a four-day-before search would also produce an SNR spike four days after the merger

There are two ingredients here, and so in the plots, we will show the product of the two.

The first is the fitting factor, this is the match of best-fitting waveform in the bank. This will be one at the time of merger, and low everywhere else

The second is the optimal SNR, i.e. the SNR you _would_ get if using a perfect-match template. For MBHB signals, this will rise rapidly at the end of the signal, and, if it is loud enough, multiply by the fitting fatcor to produce a secondary peak.
